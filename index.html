How it works

What does this do?

It fills in and signs a PDF and saves one new, completed copy named yourfile-signed.pdf. Your original file isn’t changed.

  • Fillable PDFs (forms with built-in fields, like the IRS W-9): click a field and type. Tab and Shift+Tab move between fields; Space ticks a checkbox. Your entries are saved into the PDF’s own fields.
  • Flat PDFs (no fields, e.g. a scanned form): choose Add Text, click the line, and type. Click a text box to edit it, drag it to move it, use the arrow keys to nudge it, and +/− to resize. Checkmark puts a ✓ wherever you click.
  • Undo (Ctrl+Z / ⌘Z) takes back the last change.
  • Your signature is added as a picture on the page. Text you type stays real, searchable text.

Where does my PDF go?

Nowhere. The PDF, everything you type into it, and your signature are processed by code running in this browser tab. The site is a set of static files with no server-side code, and its security policy blocks the page from sending data to any other website. Your document, entries and signature are kept in memory only (never saved to browser storage, never logged) and are cleared when you press Start over or close the tab. After the page has loaded you can turn off Wi-Fi and it still works.

The host (GitHub Pages) sees ordinary page-load requests, like any website, but never your files.

Is this a “digital signature”?

No. This is the electronic equivalent of signing a printout: it places a picture of your signature on the page. It does not add a certificate-based digital signature, verify who you are, or keep an audit trail. Whether a visible signature is acceptable depends on who you’re sending it to. This page is not legal advice.

What if the PDF is already digitally signed?

Changing a PDF that already carries a certificate-based digital signature makes that signature show as invalid or modified. The app warns you when it finds one, but that check can miss unusual files, so if a document came from an e-signature service, ask the sender before re-signing it.

Known limitations

  • Password-protected or encrypted PDFs can’t be saved. Save an unprotected copy first.
  • The signed copy is rewritten as a new file, so existing digital signatures, PDF/A conformance claims, and incremental-save history are not preserved.
  • XFA (LiveCycle) forms may not display correctly.
  • The signature is a picture on the page, not a form field.
  • Typed text uses the standard PDF font (Helvetica), which covers English and Western European letters. Other scripts and emoji can’t be saved and are left out with a notice.
  • Very large PDFs are limited by your device’s memory.
